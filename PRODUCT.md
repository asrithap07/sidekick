# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Solo knowledge workers — people managing their own day, projects, and priorities without a team workflow. They open the app to decide what to do now, capture tasks quickly, and stay on track across recurring work and longer projects.

## Product Purpose

Sidekick is a daily task and planning driver: the place someone opens each morning to see today, upcoming work, and project context — with an AI coach that helps prioritize, break down work, and stay motivated. Success looks like replacing a general-purpose todo app (Things, Todoist) for daily use, not just demoing AI features.

## Brand Personality

Warm, encouraging, trustworthy. Feels like a supportive coach beside a calm, capable tool — not a hypey AI product or a sterile enterprise dashboard. Polish and native-feeling craft (Arc/Raycast energy) without cold minimalism or gamified noise.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing-page slop: gradient heroes, big metric cards, eyebrow kickers on every section
- AI gimmick UI: bolt-on chatbot chrome, purple gradients, sparkle overload divorced from the task workflow
- Overbusy productivity apps: dashboard sprawl, fake stats, gamification theater
- Corporate admin UI: dense gray tables and enterprise clutter as the default daily surface

## Design Principles

1. **Coach in context** — AI help should feel embedded in Today, projects, and planning flows, not a separate product bolted on.
2. **Daily clarity first** — every screen should answer "what matters now?" before showing secondary metadata or insights.
3. **Warmth through craft, not decoration** — encouragement comes from copy, hierarchy, and responsive polish; not from gradient cards or metric hero templates.
4. **Native-feeling software** — interactions, spacing, and motion should feel as considered as Arc or Raycast: fast, intentional, quietly premium.
5. **Honest UI** — don't present placeholder or unverified stats as live intelligence; show only what the product actually knows.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Sensible defaults: sufficient contrast for body text, keyboard-usable controls, and reduced-motion alternatives for animations. No additional formal WCAG target beyond standard good practice unless requirements change later.
